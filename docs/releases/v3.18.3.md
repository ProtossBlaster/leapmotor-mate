# v3.18.3 — a slow charge shows its power, and a tariff keeps its decimals

Two figures that were wrong on screen while the data underneath them was right.

## What is fixed

- **The charging power on a slow charge** ([#307](https://github.com/ProtossBlaster/leapmotor-mate/issues/307),
  [@arzthilfe](https://github.com/arzthilfe)). He turned his wallbox down from 11 A to 8 A and the
  overview printed **0.00 kW** while the car was charging. His bundle says what was flowing:
  718.2 V at 1.599 A, which is **1.148 kW**. The power reading refused to compute below the
  *charge-detection* floor — the Settings value that decides whether a charge is happening at all,
  and whose help text talks about the ~11 A of a home AC charge. One threshold was doing two jobs:
  deciding **whether**, where a floor belongs, and measuring **how much**, where it wrote a zero
  over a real number. His last polls walk right through it — 3.3, 3.4, 2.0, 2.3, 1.6 A — which is
  why the reading only disappeared after he turned the current down. The floor stays where it
  decides. Zero current already gives zero power without one.

- **A four-decimal electricity tariff** ([#308](https://github.com/ProtossBlaster/leapmotor-mate/issues/308),
  [@adoewa](https://github.com/adoewa)). He pays 0.3024 €/kWh; his last charge cost him 18.10 € and
  Mate wrote 17.95. The arithmetic names the number Mate was holding: 17.95 / 0.30 = 59.83 kWh, and
  59.83 × 0.3024 = 18.09. The €/kWh field stepped in whole cents, so a browser rejected anything
  finer on submit and the price fell back to two decimals — about half a percent low on every home
  charge, and nothing said so. Four-decimal tariffs are ordinary in Europe.

## What does not change

- Nothing stored is recomputed: no charge is re-priced, and no past cost moves. A tariff typed with
  four decimals prices the charges made from now on.
- The charge-detection floor in Settings keeps its job and its default; only the power reading
  stopped obeying it.
- Regen, the stuck-counter sum and the peak-power figure each keep the guard they always had.
- The price field still refuses a negative number, and still has no ceiling — a maximum would block
  the currencies that price a kWh in tens or hundreds.

## Verification

- The full suite passes: **3 610 tests**, eleven of them new; the four that describe the defects
  were watched fail first, and the seven that describe what must not move were green from the start
  as regression guards.
- Eight mutations of the new code on a copy, all caught: the detection threshold put back into the
  power reading, either missing-signal check removed, the absolute value dropped, the kilowatt scale
  changed, a step of 0.001 (which still rejects 0.3024), the minimum removed, and a maximum added.
- Both figures were computed from the reporter's own readings rather than from an invented example.

No command was sent to any vehicle.

## Update and rollback

- Back up Mate using its existing backup feature before upgrading.
- Docker and Home Assistant use the normal image update while retaining the existing data volume.
- Nothing to do after the update. If your tariff has more than two decimals, type it again on the
  Costs page — the old field could not hold it.
- Rolling back to v3.18.2 brings both limits back and changes nothing stored.

## Italiano

**Una ricarica lenta mostra la sua potenza, e una tariffa tiene i suoi decimali.**

@arzthilfe (#307) ha abbassato la wallbox da 11 A a 8 A e la panoramica ha scritto **0,00 kW**
mentre l'auto caricava. Il suo bundle dice cosa passava davvero: 718,2 V per 1,599 A, cioè
**1,148 kW**. La potenza si rifiutava di calcolare sotto la soglia di *rilevamento ricarica* — il
valore nelle Impostazioni che serve a capire **se** una ricarica è in corso. Una soglia sola per due
mestieri: decidere **se**, dove una soglia ci sta, e misurare **quanta**, dove scriveva uno zero
sopra un numero vero. I suoi ultimi poll ci passano dentro — 3,3 · 3,4 · 2,0 · 2,3 · 1,6 A — ed è
per questo che la cifra spariva solo dopo aver abbassato la corrente. La soglia resta dov'è per
decidere: una corrente nulla dà comunque potenza nulla, senza bisogno che glielo dica.

@adoewa (#308) paga 0,3024 €/kWh; la sua ultima ricarica gli è costata 18,10 € e Mate ha scritto
17,95. Il conto dice quale numero aveva in mano Mate: 17,95 ÷ 0,30 = 59,83 kWh, e 59,83 × 0,3024 =
18,09. Il campo del prezzo saliva di centesimo in centesimo, quindi il browser rifiutava qualcosa di
più fine e il prezzo ripiegava su due decimali — mezzo punto percentuale in meno su ogni ricarica di
casa, senza che niente lo dicesse. Le tariffe a quattro decimali in Europa sono normali. **Niente
viene ricalcolato**: le ricariche già registrate tengono il loro costo, la tariffa nuova vale da
adesso. Se la tua ha più di due decimali, riscrivila nella pagina Costi: prima il campo non la
teneva.
